## 1.1 Problem

People who create projects on a Mac do the main work and also a long tail of smaller jobs around it. Today's AI can help with those jobs, but in two ways that both cost something:

- A chat. You leave your app, explain the project again, copy things across, and carry the answer back by hand.
- An agent you hand the work to. It goes away and does it. You do not see how, and you check the result afterwards.

One sentence: **the help is either somewhere else, or out of my hands.**

What supports this, and how strongly:

- About 1,200 app switches a day, about 9% of work time lost finding your place again (HBR 2022, n = 137). A small study.
- Productivity rises with up to three AI tools and falls at four or more (BCG 2026, n = 1,488).
- The two student figures that stood here (HEPI 2026, and US college use of AI) are removed. They are about students, a user Phew no longer targets.

Status: **desk research only.** No user has been interviewed. Validation with users was skipped by your decision on 10 November 2026. On 15 November a desk validation was done (`12-validation.md`). It tested the earlier working direction: teach the software, then collaborate. It did not test the direction on this page. What it found:

- The pain is real and moderately supported. Nothing kills the direction. Nothing proves it.
- Nobody was found who pays for a pointing guide, or says they would. Nobody was found refusing either.
- Nothing shows that people keep using such a tool after they have learned.
- "Show me how" is under pressure from "do it for me". This is one reason for the new direction.
- Business software training fails as a first paid niche for a Mac-only product (1.7 and `08-b2b-targets.md`).
- The biggest pools of user opinion (Reddit, review sites, the App Store) could not be read. The evidence is thin.

So the new direction, acting with you, rests on your judgement and on one finding. It has no validation of its own yet.

## 1.2 User

People who create projects on a Mac: designers, founders, makers, writers, developers. Not people who come to learn a piece of software. First user: you.

Job to be done: **work on my project with me. Take the smaller jobs I hand you, do them where I can see, and remember the project so I never explain it twice.**

The order of what he does for this user: he works with you and acts, he explains and guides, he keeps memory. See `02-vision.md` 2.6.

## 1.3 Market

- Sector: desktop AI companions and agents that see the screen and can act on it.
- Parent market: AI agents, about $7.6B to $7.8B in 2025 and about $50B to $53B by 2030. These are analyst estimates, mostly about companies. See `10-research-market.md` 10.1.
- No credible figure exists for desktop companions for individuals.
- Screen reading alone became a free macOS feature on 14 September 2026, with Siri AI in macOS 27. It works on the Mac in the EU. Only iPhone and iPad are delayed there.
- Pointing at the screen is common: at least 14 products do it, several free or open source (`11-direction.md` 11.9).
- Acting on the computer is offered by the large general assistants and by several smaller tools (1.4).
- Paid demand that could be seen sits in meetings and in company software training, not in personal companions (`11-direction.md` 11.9, takeaway 4).

## 1.5 Position

A companion that works on your projects with you, on your Mac, and remembers them.

What is an edge, and what is not (`12-validation.md` 12.7):

| Claim | What it is |
|---|---|
| He works with you, in your app, and you decide each job | The possible edge. Built in version 0.1.0, little tried, not tested with users |
| He remembers your projects, and you can see, correct and delete what he remembers | A possible edge, if the memory is right. Memory that invents things loses trust |
| He points, and is right on the current version of the app | An edge only if it is reliably right. Not measured yet (test 1 in 12.6) |
| He looks only when called. Memory is encrypted on the Mac | The price of entry, not an edge. People ask for it. Nobody was found who buys or stays because of it |
| He points at the screen | Common. At least 14 products do it |
| You can continue a conversation | Expected. Not a difference |

The honest wording of the privacy claim: memory on your Mac, thinking in the cloud. When you call him, what he reads on screen goes to the model.

The experience is the product. Memory, context, agents and the Map work underneath and are never the user's job.

## 1.6 Risks

- **Payment and staying are unproven.** Nobody was found paying for a tool like this, and nothing shows people keep using one. AI apps get first payments easily and lose subscribers sooner. Only a test with real users can answer this.
- **Acting on the Mac is a safety and trust risk.** A wrong click can delete, send or overwrite something. A page or file he reads can try to trick him with hidden instructions. So the design with its guards came first, in `docs/13-hand.md` (decision 39): what he may do and what he never does. Your decision of 12 November (acting off by default) was replaced on 15 November: acting is on from the start, with a switch in the menu to turn it off.
- **Platform makers are one feature away.** Apple, OpenAI, Anthropic and Google already ship screen view, memory and actions. Working with you step by step is a small change for them. Gemini's task mode already asks approval for each step.
- **Model cost.** A question with the Map costs about 1.7 cents today. Acting needs many looks and many calls per job. Its cost is not measured yet. It must fit under what a person would pay.
- **Scope.** This is several products in one: acting, explaining and guiding, memory, research, speech. There is no deadline, so nothing is skipped, but each part must work.
- **The hosted key.** The MVP needs your own Claude API key. An ordinary user will not create one. A key that Phew hosts needs a backend and a way to pay for it. This is a Later question, and it decides whether anyone but you can use Phew.
- **Trust.** Screen, microphone and control of other apps are the most sensitive permissions on a Mac. A small unknown maker asking for all three is a hard start.
- **The name.** Check trademark and domain for Phew before public use.

## 1.7 Who pays

Said plainly:

- Nobody was found who pays for this, or says they would.
- The people who pay for AI help today pay for the large general assistants, which are adding screen view, memory and actions.
- First paid plans of pointing tools cost $5 to $29 a month. Prices read through a summarising tool, not verified on live pages.
- Business software training was the planned first paid niche. The validation found it fails as a first niche for a Mac-only product. It is now a separate product, later (`08-b2b-targets.md`).

For now Phew is a personal project and a learning project. You are the first user. Who pays is an open question, answered only by a test with real users. The cheap tests are listed in `12-validation.md` 12.6. Which to run, and when, is decision 40.
