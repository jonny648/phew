# 8. Business software training: a separate product, later

Rewritten on 15 November 2026 for the direction you set that day. Waiting for your approval (PD.3).

## 8.1 The point

Business software training is a separate product under the same brand. It is not the Phew being built now. It comes later and needs professional expertise that the personal version does not.

What it would be: a guide for new workers who must learn proprietary or old software at work. It would explain and point on the real screen, like the personal Phew, with knowledge of that company's software and ways of working.

Nothing of it is built during the MVP. The personal version is described in `02-vision.md` and `01-foundation.md`.

Still open: decision 35 in `07-decisions.md` 7.2 asks whether this product gets its own brand. Your words of 15 November say "the same brand". Decision 35 should be closed or reworded when you approve this page.

Academic buyers stay removed (your decision of 14 November): universities and schools buying for their students are not targets.

## 8.3 What the evidence says

From the validation of 15 November (`12-validation.md` 12.1, question 1 and part c). Desk research only. No worker, buyer or IT person was interviewed.

Verdict: **as a first paid niche, with a Mac-only product, it fails as stated.**

For:

- The pain is real where it was sampled. In a KLAS survey of more than 35,000 physicians, 52% said their first training on the hospital record system was not enough. Users of business systems describe teaching themselves.
- Buyers of today's guidance tools (WalkMe, Whatfix) complain that walkthroughs are costly to write and break often. A tool that reads the live screen needs no written walkthroughs.

Against:

- Platform. Macs are about 11% of US enterprise PCs. Old business software mostly runs on Windows or through Citrix. Citrix ships a control that blocks AI screen capture, on the Mac too.
- The money that exists buys a different product: walkthroughs written centrally, installed by IT, with reports for the employer. Nothing found shows that budget would move to an assistant for each worker.
- The buyer is IT or purchasing, not the worker who suffers.
- Free lookalikes and a pointing guide bundled with Windows already exist.

A claim on this page that was wrong: "Phew needs no authoring. That is the opening." Every Mac rival that points also needs no authoring, so it is not an opening. And teaching a company's own processes does need someone to write them down (`11-direction.md` 11.9).

Limits: the voices heard were mostly developers and administrators on a few forums. No new office worker, nurse, bank clerk or government worker was heard. Searches were in English and mostly about the United States.

## 8.4 Why it comes later

- Phew is Mac only. Large companies cannot buy a tool that most of their people cannot run.
- Companies need security reviews, contracts and long buying cycles. A screen-reading tool from a small unknown maker must pass a review, while the tool from the company's existing supplier is already approved.
- Reading employees' screens raises works council and privacy questions in Europe. Whether the rules block a tool the worker calls himself, with no view for the employer, is not answered. It needs a lawyer (test 9 in `12-validation.md` 12.6).
- It needs expertise the personal version does not: knowledge of each company's software, a security pack, a data agreement, and proof that the employer cannot use it to watch staff.

## 8.5 Three questions before it is named a niche

It is not called a niche, and no market size is quoted for it, until these three are answered (`12-validation.md` 12.1 part c, and tests 7 to 10 in 12.6):

1. **Which old or proprietary software runs on Macs, and in which firms?** Cheapest way: list ten kinds of firm, find out what machines the workers use and how the software reaches them. A few phone calls.
2. **Will IT allow a screen-reading assistant from a small maker?** Cheapest way: ask three people who run IT or security what they would require. Bring a one-page drawing of where the data goes.
3. **What do new workers say?** People outside developer forums: the ones who actually sit in front of the software in their first weeks. This needs conversations, not search.

Whether to explore this product at all, and when, is your decision after the MVP (decision 26).
