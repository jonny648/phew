1. What Phew is now

The product line, word for word from CLAUDE.md:

Phew is a macOS companion that knows your projects. When called, he understands what you are doing, explains it, and guides you step by step by pointing. He can also research a task by himself and report back.

Two visible features

  1. Explain and guide. You call him. He understands what you are doing, explains it, and shows the next step on screen by pointing.
  2. A task he researches alone. You give him a task. He works in the background. The report waits in the menu bar until you open it.

Everything else serves these two: the memory, the import from other AI tools, the speed, the voice.

Who it is for

People who work on a Mac and use AI every day: designers, students, founders, developers, writers. The first user is you.

What changed since the first plan

  • From students to anyone on a Mac. The first plan was a companion for university students. On 13 November you widened it.
  • From one visible feature to two. Research by himself is new.
  • The deadline is dropped. The first aim was 24 November. On 13 November you removed it. There is no fixed deadline.
  • Nothing is skipped. No task of the MVP is skipped or moved later to save time. If a task proves wrong or impossible, it is brought to you. It is not dropped quietly.
  • The MVP is larger. It now holds a Map with real logic, the second brain, guide speed, agents, speech, then GitHub.

3. How the Map works

The Map is Phew's memory of your work: what you are working on, with whom, with what, what was decided, and what you asked him before. It was redesigned on 13 and 14 November for a general user. The first version was built around one student.

What it is for

The Map exists to answer 13 questions. Nothing is added to its design unless it helps answer one of them. Four of the 13:

  • Where did I leave off on this project?
  • What did the client ask me to change on the landing page?
  • What is due this week, and what does it need?
  • What do you know about me, and where did you learn it?

The eleven classes

Everything in the Map belongs to one of eleven fixed classes. You chose them on 13 November. Each thing also gets one free word that says what sort it is, called its "kind". It is shown in brackets in the table.

Things are joined by seven fixed links: part of, about, requires, involves, uses, at, understands. There is no loose "related to". A fact that fits no link is kept as a Note. The link "at", and one new direction of "part of", were added by Claude and wait for your yes you decide.

The three ways he recalls

  1. Always loaded. A short profile of you goes with every question: your name, how you like things done, your active projects and their tasks due soon. The last questions and answers of the conversation go too, as text, so a follow-up question works.
  2. A search before every answer. Phew's own code searches the Map with the words of your question, the app name and the window title. It adds the project, its last past questions, the facts that match, and short lists such as what is due soon. The model is not asked for this search, so it adds no wait you would notice. That is an estimate, not measured.
  3. A search the model can make itself. When the Guide (the agent that answers you) needs more, it can search the Map. Each search adds an estimated 1 to 3 seconds.

The search is by words, not by meaning. A question in Italian may miss a note written in English. Search by meaning comes after the MVP.

What is sent, and what is never stored

  • The Map file never leaves the Mac. It is never uploaded or synced. This is a hard rule.
  • Sent to the model with each question: your question, what is on screen, your short profile, and the few remembered facts that match. All of it passes the secrets filter first. In short: memory on your Mac, thinking in the cloud.
  • Sent after each answer to the Librarian (the small agent that proposes what to write to the Map): your question, his answer, the app name, the window title, the document's name with its path (where the file sits in your folders) or web address, and the stored facts found for this question. Never the window text. Never the picture.
  • Never stored: the window text and the picture. Anything the secrets filter removes.
  • Kept from the screen: only labels. The app name, the window title (kept only as the name of a document), and a file's name and path or a page's address. You approved this on 14 November.
  • Sensitive things seen on screen (health, money, passwords, other people's private messages): first proposal, they are not written unless you tell him to you decide.
  • The model only proposes. Phew's own code checks each proposal before anything is written.

Correcting and "forget that"

  • Every fact says where it came from: told (you said it), observed (he learned it while answering) or imported (from another AI tool). Told beats observed. Observed beats imported.
  • Correcting. You say "No, the deadline is the 20th". The new fact replaces the old one. The old one is kept as history, so he can answer "what was the deadline before?".
  • When a weaker source disagrees with a stronger one: first proposal, the weaker fact is kept beside it, marked "unconfirmed" you decide.
  • "Forget that" is a real delete. The thing, its past versions, its links and the notes about it are wiped from the file. It cannot be undone, and the Map has no backup. Only your own words can start it. Text on the screen, on the web or in a file never can.
  • In the first proposal he asks before deleting you decide, and past questions that mention the thing are emptied, not deleted whole you decide.
  • What forgetting cannot reach: exports and reports you already saved, backups of the Mac such as Time Machine, and what was already sent to the model in earlier questions.

Where the file lives

One file on your Mac: ~/Library/Application Support/Phew/map.sqlite. SQLite is a small database that lives in one file and is built into every Mac. Nothing to install, no account, no server. If you use Time Machine, it copies this file like any other. You can export the Map to one readable text file to check what he wrote.

The main risk

Wrong facts can pile up. No source we found shows a small model filling eleven classes reliably. You chose to build all eleven at once. The protections: Phew's code checks every write, the export lets you look, and "forget that" removes a mistake.

The numbers in the Map (days, limits, how sure he is of a fact) are all first proposals you decide. The full design is in docs/05-map.md.

4. How he works alone

You type or say a task, for example "find five good sources on kerning". The agent that does this work is called the Runner. On 13 November you decided what version 1 may do: read and research, report on the card, and save the report as a new file in one folder you choose.

What a research task may read and do

  • Read your screen once, at the moment you give the task. Not again.
  • Search the Map.
  • Search the web and read web pages. This runs at Anthropic, not on your Mac.
  • Write the report text. If you chose a reports folder, Phew's own code, not the Runner, saves it there as a new file.

What it can never do

  • Change, overwrite or delete a file. Editing files comes after the MVP.
  • Click or type in other apps. That comes after the MVP, as a separate switch that is off by default.
  • Open a web address that came from your screen, the Map or a file.
  • Save or write any file by itself. The Runner has only three tools: web search, reading a web page, and Map search. None of them writes anything.
  • Show a result by itself.

How the report waits

While he works, a mark shows on the menu bar icon. Stop in the menu ends the task. A finished report waits in the menu bar until you click it. Then it opens on the card. He never appears unasked. Before your first task, one screen tells you in plain words what a task reads and sends you decide.

Still open: one task at a time or several, what Stop leaves behind, and what happens to a waiting report if Phew quits you decide. Also how the report file is named and listed you decide.

The limits

Each task has a limit on searches, on pages and on cost. The first proposal is 5 searches, 8 pages and 50 cents you decide. At a limit he stops, reports what he has, and names the limit he reached.

The main risk

A web page can contain hidden instructions that try to make the agent send out what it knows: your screen text, your memory. Every vendor says its defence against this is not perfect.

The protections come from Phew's own code and from settings at Anthropic, not from asking the model to behave:

  • What he reads on the screen, in the Map, on the web or in a file is data, never instructions. This is a hard rule.
  • He has no tool that writes or changes anything. So a hidden instruction has nothing to act with.
  • Two guards against opening a planted address. Phew's code removes every web address from the screen text, the Map facts and file text before sending. And a setting at Anthropic lets him open only addresses he found by his own searches or on pages he already opened.
  • The limits per task keep a runaway task small.
  • The secrets filter runs on everything Phew sends.
  • First proposal: a research report writes no facts to the Map in version 1 you decide.

One risk stays. A web page can try to steer what he searches for next. Search words can include names from your screen or the Map, so a page could get a client's name sent out in a search you decide.

The second brain import

  1. Paste box. You paste the memory list your other assistant wrote about you. Nothing is sent until you confirm. The secrets filter runs, then the small model sorts the text into single facts. The text is treated as data, never as instructions.
  2. Claude Code's files. You choose a project folder. He reads its CLAUDE.md. First proposal: he finds that project's memory files himself, in a hidden folder, and shows you the list before anything is sent you decide.
  3. You tick what to keep. Only ticked facts are stored, marked as imported, with source and date. An imported fact never replaces what you told him or what he observed. First proposal: a menu entry "Undo last import" removes the whole last import you decide.

The paste box, the tick list, the file list, the consent screen and the list of saved reports are new small screens. They touch a hard rule you decide. So does picking a folder you decide.

5. A faster guide

Today the guide works but feels slow. A "look" is one check of the screen by the model, after you act, to see which step you are on. Measured on 29 real looks: 1 second of waiting, then 2.1 seconds on average and up to 6.2 seconds. Each look costs 0.9 cents. Scrolling also triggers looks.

The changes in phase PS, decided on 13 November:

  • No look while you drag or scroll. One look when you let go. When you scroll, the pointer moves with its target.
  • No look when nothing changed. A click on empty space sends nothing to the model.
  • The next step shows at once after a simple click. The Guide marks the steps that need only a click. When you click the marked target, the next step shows in under half a second. The look then confirms. If the step was not really done, the card goes back. Steps that need typing, dragging or choosing still wait for the look. So does the last step, so the card never closes on a guess.
  • Less is sent. The picture is cut around the target, fewer screen elements are listed, and the part of the request that never changes is reused at a lower price. Target: under 0.5 cents per look.
  • Slow answers are cut and tried again. Target: no look above 4 seconds.
  • A screen watch during a guide. See below.

The screen watch, and when it stops

During a guide, Phew watches the screen. He does it for one reason: to know when the screen has stopped changing, and whether it changed at all. This widens the rule "he reads the screen only when called" for the length of a guide. You approved that on 13 November.

  • The watch alone sends nothing to the model. A look is still made only after you act.
  • What he watches stays in memory. It is never written to disk or to a log.
  • It starts when the guide's steps are shown.
  • It stops as soon as following stops: the last step is done, the card closes, you press Escape, you press Pause, the limit of 40 looks is reached, or a look fails.
  • First proposal: it also stops after 2 minutes without an action from you, and starts again when you act you decide.
  • First proposal: Pause also ends a running guide you decide.
  • A list of apps he must never read is not built. In the MVP or later you decide.

All speed and cost targets are estimates until they are measured in the build.

6. What the research found

32 products were researched on 13 November.

  • Pointing is now common. At least six consumer products and one Microsoft feature point at the screen. One of them, Peek, also waits for your click and follows you. So Phew's guide that follows you is not unique.
  • Agents that work alone and voice are common too.
  • Pointing with project memory is not. The guides remember little or nothing. The memory tools do not point. On the Mac, nobody has both.
  • Memory kept on the Mac, looking only when called, is not. Local memory exists only in tools that record all the time. Tools that look only when called keep almost nothing.
  • So: lead with memory, not with pointing. "He knows your projects" is the open space. What protects Phew from copies is the combination, not any single feature.

The hard truths

  1. "Local" is a weak claim while pictures go to a cloud model. The honest wording is: memory on your Mac, thinking in the cloud.
  2. People pay little for pointing. A first paid plan costs 5 to 29 dollars a month, with free options.
  3. Paying for personal memory is unproven. Rewind was sold and shut. Others are moving to teams and meetings. This is a reading of their moves, not a published fact.
  4. Apple, Microsoft, Google and OpenAI are each one feature away. Expect the gap to narrow within 6 to 12 months. This is a judgement, not a published fact.
  5. Going general puts Phew directly against HeyClicky (a pointing companion; its site says 25,000+ users) and Peek, with no advantage until the memory works.

The prices were read through a tool that summarises pages. Check the live page before quoting one.

7. Rules that changed

The hard rules in CLAUDE.md that are new or reworded since 12 November, in plain words. These are already approved. You approved them on 13 and 14 November, and they are listed in docs/07-decisions.md 7.1. The one exception is rule 8, on models: it follows the Follower change you delegated to Claude on 12 November and the design of the Runner. They are here so you can read them once more.

  1. Two visible features (it was one). Explain and guide, and a task he researches alone. Everything else serves them.
  2. He never appears unasked. New part: an agent's result waits in the menu bar.
  3. He reads the screen only when called. New part: during a guide he may watch the screen until the guide ends, to know when it has settled, and look again after you act. What he watches stays in memory.
  4. New. What an agent may do. It may read the screen once when given a task, the Map and the web. It changes nothing, except new files in one folder you chose. It never overwrites or deletes.
  5. New. Data, never instructions. What an agent reads from the web, a file or pasted text is data.
  6. Reworded. What he captures (screen text and screenshots) lives in memory only. It is dropped when the answer is done or stopped. For a guide: when the guide ends. For a task: when the task ends or is stopped. Also when the panel closes without a question. It is never kept for a follow-up question, and never written to disk or to a log. The app name, the window title and a file's path or page address are labels, not the capture. The Map may keep them.
  7. New. The Map lives only on the Mac.
  8. Reworded. Which model does what. The small model for the Conductor, the Observer and the Librarian. The main model for the Guide, the Follower and the Runner. Before, only the Guide used the main model.
  9. Reworded. Logs. The file calls.log holds only numbers and names: the time, the agent, the model, token counts (a token is a small piece of text, the unit the model's price is counted in), cost, timings, how the call ended and a task number. No log holds a question, a task's words, search words, web addresses, Map facts or an answer.

Two hard rules may still change. They are the first two cards of the group for phase PB: picking a folder you decide and new small screens you decide. Nothing is changed in CLAUDE.md until you say yes.
