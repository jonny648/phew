Phew! costs

16 November 2026, version 0.1.0. What Phew costs to run today, what it would cost with many users, and how to cut it without losing anything. Nothing on this page is built. Each number is measured (from the call log or a logged check), computed from measured numbers, or estimated; the text says which.

Three things to keep in mind.

  1. The biggest cost is what he sends, not what he writes. Each call sends a fresh picture of the screen, its text and its list of controls to the main model. That is 58% of all money spent, and about 70% of one question. The picture alone is about 30%.
  2. The main model costs 20 times the small one. The three agents on the small model are 4% of the spend together. Ideas that only touch them cannot save more than 4%.
  3. No real use has been measured yet. Almost all 2,091 calls in the log are tests on made-up data. The monthly figures rest on assumed days. A week of real use would replace them.

Two ways to run it

Each user brings their own key (today)

The model cost is theirs. Your cost per user is zero. Nothing passes through a server of yours. The price is friction: a person must create an API key before the first answer. That is what stops the first ten testers.

You pay for everyone (a hosted key)

A small server holds one key, checks who is asking and sets a daily limit. From about $25 a month to run. The model cost above becomes yours. Screens would pass through your server: it must keep nothing, and "calls only the Claude API" stops being literally true. Your decision.

Price for a margin of 70% on the model cost: $34 a month today, $19.40 after the plan. At $5 or $10 a month a hosted key loses money on today's costs.

One runaway user

The brakes that exist: a research task stops at 50¢, 5 searches and 5 minutes; three tasks at once; a job stops at 40 actions; a guide at 40 looks. There is no limit on questions a day or on money a day. Three tasks restarted without pause would cost about $18 an hour (estimated). With the user's own key that is their money. With a hosted key it is yours, so a daily limit per user must come before a hosted key.

The ideas, by family

Send less. His fixed instructions for an explanation are about 2,190 tokens, and about 1,490 of them are the text about himself. They are cheap while the cache is warm and 25 times dearer when it is cold, which is half the time for someone who calls him now and then. Sending that text only for questions about Phew saves about 0.19¢ a question. A half-size picture when the window already gave 1,500 characters of text saves about 0.29¢ on perhaps 6 questions in 10. Guides and jobs keep the full picture: they point at pixels. Trimming the conversation history is not worth it: 8 earlier turns cost 0.03¢, because they are reused from the cache.

Ask less often. Today a job makes one call per action plus one. One call that returns up to five actions, each with what should be visible after it, would turn 6 calls into about 3. The existing guards (control gone, control moved, a surprise window, the same action three times) stop the job when the plan goes stale, so the failure is "stopped", not "did the wrong thing". In a guide, after a clean click on a click-only step, the code can read the window again and confirm the step itself when the next control has appeared. Folding the routing into the answer saves 0.2%: that one is about speed, not cost.

Use the cheaper model more. This is where the largest saving is, and the largest risk. A look answered by the small model costs about 0.02¢ against 0.41¢. But on 12 November the small model followed 3 guides of 4 where the main one followed 4 of 4, and it missed exactly "two steps done at once". For explanations, the answer is the first thing a person sees, and the checks measure whether it is right, not whether it reads well. Both ideas are last in the order, and each has a gate it must pass.

Use what is already paid for. The cache lives 5 minutes today. A person who calls him every 15 minutes pays to write the fixed part again each time: 0.77¢ on top of 1.3¢. The one-hour cache costs more to write and nothing to keep alive, so one write can serve a whole working day. It is worse for someone who asks five times a day, so the code would choose per user from the times of their last calls. With a hosted key the fixed instructions are the same for everyone and would stay warm for all: up to 10% of the month.

Do it on the Mac for free. Apple's on-device language model exists on this macOS, answers in a fixed form, and knows Italian. It holds only 4,096 tokens, and it can only replace the small-model calls, which are 4% of the spend. It is a good idea for privacy and speed, and worth almost nothing for cost. The real free wins are rules in code: the two "ask less often" ideas above.

Remember answers. The same question on the same screen could be answered from the last answer. Repeats are probably rare, a drawing can change while its text stays the same, and keeping a fingerprint of the screen after the answer bends the rule that a capture is never kept. Not worth it.

Structure. Keep "bring your own key" as the free or cheap way in, and make the hosted key the paid plan. A heavy user costs ten times a light one, so a flat price with no limit lets one user in ten eat the margin: at $15 a month a light user leaves $11 and a heavy one loses $24. A daily allowance about the size of a normal day, with research tasks and long jobs counted separately, keeps that in hand. Using a person's own Claude subscription to carry the calls would make the cost zero, but whether that is allowed is not verified: do not plan on it.

What to measure first, because the estimate is uncertain

  1. The real time between a person's calls. It decides the cache idea: 5% or nothing.
  2. The real size of a question. The 1.3¢ was measured on the largest capture he can make. A real window may cost half. That would lower every number on this page, today's included.
  3. Looks per guide, and how many are already skipped.
  4. The small model's score on the guide and page checks. This one spends a little money and needs your word.
  5. Calls per job in real apps. The 0.33¢ comes mostly from the practice window, which has little on screen. A real call may cost 0.7¢.

9. A "skin" for each user: what is possible

Research of 16 November 2026, on your question: can the model be changed, or given a lasting layer per user, so it does not have to load and understand the memory on every request? Nothing here is built.

Short answer. The model itself cannot be changed: Claude cannot be trained on a user through the API today. But the same effect exists, and Phew already uses half of it: a block of text that the model has already read and that is reused at one twentieth of the price. Your "skin" is that block, one per user. It is worth building, more for speed and quality than for money, because the memory is not where the money goes. The money goes to the screen.

Why the model cannot be edited

  • Training Claude on your own data is not offered. The one route that existed (an older small model, through Amazon) has closed. Secondary sources; Anthropic's own pages were not read for this.
  • It would not fit anyway. A trained copy per user costs far more than any saving, your memory changes every day while training takes hours, and it would put your private facts inside a model kept on someone else's servers.
  • A model has no lasting memory between two calls. Every call starts from nothing, and everything it should know must be in the call. What can change is the price and the speed of the part that repeats.

The idea that works: layers that are already read

The model's provider keeps the start of a request "already read" for 5 minutes or one hour. Reused, it costs $0.10 per million tokens in place of $2.00. Only the start can be kept: the first byte that differs ends the reuse. So the order decides everything, and a request can hold four such layers.

The real gain is not money. Today he knows you only when he thinks of searching. With the skin he knows you in every answer, at once, in guides and jobs too, where no memory is sent today. A spoken name is heard right more often, because the names are in front of him. That is the part of your idea that is right, and it is cheap.

The limit, said plainly: the memory is about 4% of what Phew costs. A perfect skin cannot save more than that. What costs is the screen: 58%.

The same idea, aimed at the screen: a skin for each app

This is where your idea can save real money. Today, for every call, he is sent up to 80 controls of the window and a picture, because he knows nothing about the app beforehand. An app card would be written once per app and version: what the app is, where its menus and panels are, what its controls are called, how its common jobs go. Kept as a layer, already read.

  • With the card, a call can send fewer controls and a smaller picture, because he already knows the place. Estimated: 5 to 10% of the month, uncertain until measured.
  • It also makes him better at acting in that app, which is the weak point named in the next roadmap (Dia, Figma, Fusion).
  • The cards hold nothing personal, so with a hosted key they would be shared by every user and almost free.
  • Cost: writing a card is one research task per app, a few cents, and it goes stale when the app changes. That needs a date on each card and a way to notice.

What does not work, so you need not wonder

  • Uploading your profile once as a file. It is still read and charged on every call, unless kept as a layer. And it would sit on their servers. No gain over the skin.
  • A small trained add-on per user, on the Mac. Possible in a lab, on open models. It could only replace the small-model jobs, which are 4% of the cost.
  • Keeping the skin alive for days. One hour is the longest. After a night he reads it again, once, for 0.6¢.

What I suggest, in order

  1. Your skin and the project's skin, as layers 2 and 3, with the warm-up. Small work. It changes how he feels more than any other item on this page.
  2. App cards, as part of the next acting phase (N2). This is the one that can cut the screen cost.
  3. "The screen has not changed", if you allow the fingerprint.
  4. Holding the conversation at the provider: only if speed in guides and jobs still bothers you after the rest.

One rule the skin must keep: nothing in a layer may change between two calls, or the reuse is lost and it costs more than today. So no time of day, no last question, no counter inside it. And layer 1 must never hold anything personal, because with a hosted key it would be shared by everyone.
