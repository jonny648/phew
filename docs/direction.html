Why this page

After your P5 hand tests you found Phew too general. "A companion that understands you and collaborates" names no job. You asked for one specific job, with a method, inside the MVP.

Four directions were researched. Then a reviewer tried to kill each one. None survived as first written. Three of the four reviews ended at the same place, shown in the recommendation below.

Two mixes

Mix 1. Learn one pro Mac app by doing your own work in it

A narrowed, plus D's teaching method. C's studio packs come later.

  • Phew points at each step in one chosen app, checks you did it, and remembers what you can now do alone.
  • From D: a hint before the answer, and a short recall check.
  • Later, from C: a studio hands its own processes to juniors as a "studio pack" file.
  • The Map becomes the product: a record of what you mastered. No pointing tool keeps one (a judgement, not a published fact).

Recommended Three reviews and the competitor refresh all point here.

Mix 2. Pick up a project where you left it

B plus the guide.

  • "Where was I on this project" answered from the Map, then the next step shown by pointing in the real app.
  • Adds Park (save where you stopped) and a blocked or waiting-on state.
  • Uses everything built, and the pointing twist is unique.
  • But no evidence that projects stall on "how do I do this here".

Fallback Test it the same way if Mix 1 fails its test.

Recommendation: Mix 1

You open your app, ask how, and Phew shows you. Next time he knows you already did it.

Why

  • The only direction where what is built (guide, following clicks, the Map) is the job itself.
  • The Map stops being background and becomes the product: what you did alone, with help, or got stuck on.
  • Paid demand exists in software training.
  • Privacy still holds: looks only when called, memory on the Mac.
  • A business path stays open (studio packs), with no server.

What the MVP must add

  1. The chosen app, a recorded test set of its common tasks, and a bar of about 95% correct pointing. Recordings stay outside the repository.
  2. The chat card (PD.4), so a lesson runs over several turns.
  3. Mastery in the Map: each skill marked done alone, done with help, or stuck. New Map classes.
  4. The teaching method: try first, hint before the answer, a short recall check. Help that fades as you improve.
  5. A progress panel you open yourself from the menu.
  6. Phase PS kept: direct hits under 0.5 s, about 3 cents or less per guide.
  7. App version awareness, so Phew never points at a menu from an older release.

Proposed for Later (you decide)

  • The research task (phase PA) as a general feature. Could stay narrowed to "research how to do this in my app".
  • The second brain (phase PB), unless the app is Xcode.
  • Studio packs and any business version.
  • Voice (P6) stays: hands are busy in pro apps.

Rules this would change

  • 02-vision 2.7 "Not a chat app": the chat card makes Phew partly a chat.
  • CLAUDE.md "Two visible features": the second may move to Later.
  • Map classes: new ones for skills and mastery.
  • "He never appears unasked": only if you allow a quiet "review waiting" mark. Not needed for the MVP.

Biggest risk

People learn the app, then stop paying. AI apps already lose subscribers about 30% faster than other apps. Second risk: pointing accuracy in a dense pro app.

Cheap test, before building the new parts

  1. You as user zero: two weeks learning one candidate app with today's build. Log each guide as right or wrong. A few dollars of API.
  2. A recorded set of about 30 common tasks in 2 or 3 candidate apps. Keep the app that reaches about 95%.
  3. 10 to 20 interviews with self-taught learners of that app.
  4. Ask 20 of them to pre-pay $10 to $15 a month, or a one-off fee.
  5. If accuracy stays under the bar, or fewer than 20 pre-pay, test Mix 2 the same way.
