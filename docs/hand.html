Phew! acting design

Phase PH "Hand", task PH.1. Updated on 15 November 2026 to your decisions of that day. Approved with the plan of that day. How Phew does things for you on the Mac. No acting code exists yet. Full version with sources: docs/13-hand.md.

In one line: when your words ask for doing, he does the whole job by himself, lists each action on the card, and stops the moment anything is not as expected. No buttons, no questions.

  1. You call him and ask, as today. There is no "Do it" button. Your words decide.
  2. He does the whole job. No approval per step. He does not pause before send, delete, publish or replace.
  3. A mark shows while he acts. Each action is listed on the card as it happens. One that cannot be undone carries a mark in the list.
  4. After each action he looks again to check it worked, before the next one.
  5. Stop always works, within one second. Touching the app yourself also stops him.
  6. Several apps in one job: he brings another app to the front, works there, and returns you to where you were.

When something is not as expected (the button moved, you switched app, a dialog appeared that the job did not call for, the check sees no change) he stops and says so. He never tries again blind.

What he may do

When you work with him there are two levels. Showing (pointing, highlighting) is always allowed.

LevelRuleExamples
ActsEverything your words asked for. No confirmMake a word bold, tick a reminder, fill cells, pick a tool, open a tab. Also send, delete, publish and replace, when that is the job
NeverNot done, whatever the words. He points insteadPasswords and secure fields, paying and card details, sign-in and account settings, System Settings, Terminal and code editors, password managers, any app on your "Never read" list, Phew's own windows

Who decides the level: Phew's own code, never the AI model. Nothing on the screen takes a thing off the Never list.

The code still knows which actions cannot be undone. You are not asked about them. They are marked in the card's list.

Research agents (phase PA) act in the background, only through ways that do not take your screen: the apps' own scripting and the system's calendar and reminders access. Notes, Reminders, Calendar, Excel, new files. No clicking in the background. For an agent, anything that cannot be undone or that leaves your Mac waits in the menu bar for one confirm from you.

The guards that remain

All in one place in the code. They need no effort from you.

  1. Text on your screen is information, never orders. Only your own words start a job.
  2. The Never list, decided by code, in every app he touches.
  3. Nothing is ever typed into a password field.
  4. No action in an app on your "Never read" list.
  5. He stops by himself on anything unexpected, and never retries blind.
  6. Stop within one second. Touching the app yourself stops him.
  7. A runaway brake: a ceiling on actions per job (first proposal 40) and per minute. It is not there to slow a normal job.
  8. One acting function. A build test fails if anything else in the app can act.
  9. Off until you press Allow on the consent screen.
  10. Every action listed on the card, a mark while he acts.
  11. Every action logged on your Mac as numbers and fixed words only. Never the text, never a name.

Removed by your decisions: one action per approval, the 60-second approval, the question per app, the run of at most 5 steps.

Said plainly about guard 1: with no approval step, code can no longer hold it by itself. The model plans the steps, and code cannot tell a step you meant from a step a trick planted. The other guards limit what a tricked step can do. The trick test (PH.13) measures it.

What can still go wrong

macOS will not stop any of this: the permission Phew already has is enough to click and type anywhere. Only his own code stands in between.

What "never confirm" means, said once. Text on a web page or in a shared document can be written to trick an AI. He can also simply get a step wrong. With no confirm, a tricked or mistaken step, including a send or a delete, happens before you can stop it. This is your choice, and the design is built on it.

  1. Something on your screen steers him, and he does it. No company has solved this. A trick cannot start a job, only bend one you started. Passwords, paying, sign-in and settings are never touched. What is not stopped: a planted "send this" inside a job you asked for. Web pages are the riskiest place.
  2. He gets it wrong by himself. He takes "show" for "do", or deletes the wrong row. The first wrong action has already happened when you read it on the card.
  3. A harmful button with a harmless name. "Next" on a web page that pays or places an order. Before, it would have been asked. Now it is done.
  4. He clicks the wrong thing. Mostly on a canvas (Figma, Fusion), where there is no button to compare, only a place.
  5. Undo may not bring it back. Figma and Fusion save to the cloud. A message or an invitation has already gone out.
  6. An agent acts while you are elsewhere. A tricked agent can still add wrong notes, reminders, events or sheets. What cannot be undone waits for you.
  7. Work across apps. He brings an app to the front while you may be typing. He also looks at, and acts in, an app you did not have in front when you called him.

What limits the damage: the Never list, stopping on anything unexpected, Stop and your own touch, the list on the card, the ceiling on actions, off until Allow.

The consent screen

Phew can do things for you.

When you ask, he clicks, types and picks menus in your apps by himself, and lists what he did. He never types passwords, pays, or changes system settings. Stop always works.

Text on a web page or in a document can mislead him. He may then do a wrong thing, including sending or deleting.

[Allow] [Not now]

Shown once, before his first action. One consent covers all apps. After Allow, a switch "Let Phew act" appears in the menu.

Rules in CLAUDE.md this changes

Four bent:

  • "An agent changes nothing, except new files": he changes your apps when you ask. Agents act in the background, in the narrow way above.
  • "Nothing from Later is built in the MVP": actions in other apps move into the MVP.
  • "He reads the screen only when called": he also looks after each of his own actions, to check it.
  • "He never activates the app or takes focus from the front app": working across apps brings another app to the front during a job you asked for. Phew's own panels still never take focus.

Two privacy rules added to, none removed: a second local log for actions, numbers and fixed words only; and password fields are never written to, as well as never read.

Build order: the guards, the consent screen and a made-up practice window come first. Nothing acts on a real app before task PH.5. Phase PA then adds the agents.
