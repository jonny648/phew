Phew! validation

Phase PD, task PD.7. 15 November 2026. Your working direction, tested against what users and customer-feedback articles say. Full report with every quote and link: docs/12-validation.md.

Verdict: the direction is weakened, not killed.

The pain is real. Nothing shows that people will pay, or that they stay after learning. One part fails as you wrote it: business training on old software as the first paid niche.

The three parts of your direction

Teach the software you work in

Mixed

The need is supported. Learners say tutorials don't match the screen in front of them and controls are hard to find. Chat assistants invent menu items.

The paying part is unknown. Nobody was found who pays for a pointing tutor, or who says they would. Nobody refusing either.

Reading

A way in for people learning hard tools (3D, CAD, design). Not a lasting position by itself.

Keep collaborating after learning

Not supported

Your answer to "they learn, then cancel". No evidence was found for it or against it. It has not failed; nothing tested it.

Three hints against

  • A user spoke of pointing help as something needed "when I was learning", in the past.
  • An ERP user says the learning pain passes "after a few months".
  • Collaboration is where ChatGPT, Claude and Copilot are strongest, and people already pay for them.

Reading

A hypothesis to test, not a reason the business works.

Business training on old software, as first paid niche

Fails as stated
  • Macs are about 11% of company computers in the US. Old work software runs on Windows or through Citrix.
  • Citrix can block AI screen capture, on Mac too.
  • The $30,000 to $40,000 a year that firms spend buys a different product: guides written centrally, installed by IT, with reports for the employer.
  • The buyer is IT, not the worker who suffers. A screen-reading tool from a small vendor must pass a security review.

Reading

Keep it as a later option with its own research, as you already planned. Do not call it the first paid niche yet.

What is left as Phew's edge

  1. Pointing that is right, on the current version of the app. This is the complaint people have about tutorials and chat assistants. It supports your plan to research each app before use. It also warns: that research goes stale, and stale is the very pain being sold against.
  2. Privacy that is shown, not promised. Let people see what he remembers, where an answer came from, and delete it.

Both can only be proven by a test, not by more reading.

The cheapest tests, in order

  1. Accuracy. 20 real tasks per app. Run Phew on each, count right the first time. Ask a general chat assistant the same 20 and compare. No users needed.
  2. Read what the research could not open. Reddit, the Autodesk forum, App Store and software-directory reviews of the closest rivals. By hand, by you or with me in the browser.
  3. Staying. Interview 10 people who learned a hard app in the last two years. What do they use now? When did they last need help?
  4. Paying. Ask a real price of 10 to 20 learners. A promise to pay does not count.
  5. Against the big assistants. The same 10 tasks with Phew and with an assistant that sees the screen. Five people say which they would keep.
  6. For the business niche, later: which old software runs on Macs and where; ask 3 IT people what they would require; one hour with a lawyer on EU monitoring rules.

Suggested changes. None is decided

  • Present teaching as the way in for people learning hard tools. Do not present it as proven to pay.
  • Write "collaboration stops cancelling" as something to test.
  • Move business training on old software from "first paid niche" to "later, with its own research".
  • Decide what "collaborate" means when users ask him to act. Today he changes nothing in other apps.
  • Keep "never guide unasked". Pop-up guidance is hated.
  • Keep the privacy rules. Say in one sentence on the consent screen that the screen goes to a cloud model when called.
  • Uninstall cleanly. A rival is rated 2.7 of 5 largely for staying on the machine.

Limits of this research

  • Reddit, G2, Capterra, the Mac App Store and the Autodesk forums refused to load. The biggest pools of user opinion were not read.
  • Most voices are developers, ERP administrators and hobby machinists. No new office worker, nurse or clerk was heard.
  • English only, mostly about the United States. No Italian sources.
  • Quotes were checked on their pages by a second pass, but they are short excerpts. Check by hand before using one in public.
  • Reading can show a pain exists. It cannot show that people pay or stay.
