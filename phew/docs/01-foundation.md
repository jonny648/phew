# 1. Foundation

## 1.1 Problem

Students use AI every day and now spend real effort managing it: choosing the tool, explaining the context again, moving text and screenshots, switching apps, deciding between too many options behind a chat box.

One sentence: **the AI lives somewhere else and does not know what I am doing.**

Numbers from the research:

- 95% of UK undergraduates use AI. 61% use it to have concepts explained (HEPI 2026, n = 1,054).
- 85% of US college students used AI for coursework in a year.
- About 1,200 app toggles a day, about 9% of work time lost reorienting (HBR 2022, n = 137).
- Productivity rises with up to three AI tools and falls at four or more (BCG 2026, n = 1,488).

Status: desk research only. Validation with users was skipped by your decision on 10 November 2026.

## 1.2 User

University students, 18 to 26, on a Mac, using AI daily for coursework and their own projects. First user: you.

Job to be done: help me understand and move forward on what is in front of me, without leaving it and without explaining my course again.

## 1.3 Market

- Sector: screen-aware desktop AI companions, inside AI assistants and agents.
- Parent market: AI agents, about $7.6B to $7.8B in 2025, about $50B to $53B by 2030. Mostly enterprise. No credible figure for consumer companions.
- Screen reading alone became a free macOS feature on 14 September 2026.

## 1.4 Competitors

| Product | Overlap | What Phew does differently |
|---|---|---|
| Clicky | Cursor companion that sees the screen and points | Phew is silent, lives in the notch, knows your courses, listens to lectures |
| Littlebird | Always-on context and work memory | Built for professionals. No guiding on screen, no character |
| Siri AI | On-screen awareness, free | No course memory, no guiding, no lecture capture |
| ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini desktop | General help with screen context | A chat to manage. Phew removes the chat |
| MemoryPlugin, Supermemory | Shared memory | Memory only, no experience |

## 1.5 Position

Clicky for students, built around what they actually do: courses, lectures, assignments, projects. The experience is the product. Memory, context, agents and the Map work underneath and are never the user's job.

## 1.6 Risks

- Scope. This is five products in one. The MVP in `06-sprint.md` carries only the main one.
- Apple, Clicky or Littlebird ships a student version.
- Trust: screen, microphone and actions in other apps are the three most sensitive permissions on a Mac.
- Model cost per student against what a student pays.
- Students rarely pay for tools. See 1.7.
- The name: check trademark and domain for Phew before public use.

## 1.7 Who pays

Students and early founders are heavy users and weak payers. As a consumer product this would likely need to be free or open source. The paying versions are business ones, where an organisation buys and an individual uses. See `08-b2b-targets.md`.

For now this does not change the build. Phew is a personal tool and a learning project. The student is still the user the MVP is designed for.
